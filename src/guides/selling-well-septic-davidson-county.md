Well and septic systems deserve a separate part of your selling plan in Davidson County. Gather what you know, identify what remains unclear, and avoid making claims the records don’t support.

## Which records should you gather?

Gather available permits, repair and service records, system locations, and any test reports. Ask county Environmental Health about records for the actual parcel. Keep a clear distinction between documented information and what someone remembers.

## Should you arrange system checks before listing?

Discuss appropriate inspections and testing with qualified professionals before deciding what to market. The right scope depends on the system and property. Ask what the results establish and what remains outside the inspection.

## What should you avoid claiming?

Avoid promising system capacity, suitability for an addition, or approval for a different use without confirmation. Ask the relevant authority and professionals about your actual plan. Yard size alone doesn’t answer a septic question.

## How does this affect preparation for buyers?

Organize the records and explain known concerns accurately through your agent. Allow for access and specialist scheduling. Buyers can make better decisions when the information is clear and property-specific.

## Reduce surprises with reliable system records

Gather available septic permits, well records, repair invoices, and inspection history. Ask Davidson County officials or qualified professionals about documented capacity and proposed changes; do not assume a system supports an addition. Decide with your agent whether pre-listing assessments would help buyers evaluate the property.
