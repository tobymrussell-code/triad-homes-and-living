I meet a lot of Florida transplants in the Triad. Some come for jobs, some to be near family, and plenty just did the math and liked what they saw. The move goes smoothly once you know what's different, and a couple of North Carolina rules catch Florida buyers off guard almost every time.

## What changes when you move from Florida to North Carolina?

**Seasons.** The Triad has all four. Winters are mild compared with up north, but you'll see the occasional snow or ice day. Summers are warm and humid. Spring brings a heavy coat of yellow pine pollen on everything you own for a few weeks.

**Taxes.** North Carolina has a flat state income tax, which Florida doesn't have. Property taxes depend on the county, and homes inside city limits pay a city rate on top. Run the numbers on the actual address. My [affordability guide](/guides/how-much-house-can-i-afford-davidson-county/) shows how.

**Hurricanes.** The Triad is well inland, but storm remnants can still bring heavy rain and wind. Get insurance quotes early in your search, not the week before closing.

**Barbecue.** North Carolina has opinions. Around here, Lexington-style pork with a vinegar-based red dip is the local standard.

## What surprises Florida buyers about buying a home in NC?

This is where people lose money, so pay attention. This isn't legal advice, just how it works in real life.

### North Carolina is a buyer-beware state

Sellers fill out a disclosure, but they can answer "No Representation" on many items. That means they aren't promising anything either way. Your inspection is how you find out what you're buying. Read the [NC buyer-beware guide](/guides/north-carolina-buyer-beware/).

### Due diligence costs money

In Florida, you may be used to an inspection contingency. In North Carolina, you negotiate a due diligence period and pay the seller a **due diligence fee** for it. During that window you can inspect, appraise, and back out for almost any reason. If you walk away, you generally get your earnest money back, but the due diligence fee stays with the seller. In competitive situations, a stronger due diligence fee helps your offer stand out. Read [due diligence fees in NC](/guides/due-diligence-fees-nc/).

### Attorneys close the deal

North Carolina uses real estate attorneys to handle title work and closings. See [closing costs in North Carolina](/guides/closing-costs-north-carolina/).

### You'll sign a buyer agreement before touring

Agents have to have a written agreement with you before showing homes. See [buyer agency agreements in NC](/guides/buyer-agency-agreements-nc/).

## Focus on the big items in the inspection

North Carolina inspection reports can be long, and most of the items are minor. Focus on the expensive ones: the roof, structural issues, HVAC, and moisture in crawlspaces, which are common here and something many Florida buyers have never dealt with. Read [home inspection in North Carolina](/guides/home-inspection-north-carolina/).

## Which Triad town should a Florida transplant pick?

Start with the map, not the listing photos. If you're working in Lexington, you might also look at Thomasville, Welcome, Wallburg, and rural Davidson County. If you want bigger-city amenities, look at Winston-Salem, High Point, and Greensboro.
