Yes, you can sell your own house in North Carolina. People do it every year. The real question is whether it saves you money once you account for everything you'll be handling yourself. Here's the honest rundown.

## What does selling FSBO actually involve?

When you sell "for sale by owner," you take on the agent's job:

- **Pricing.** Pricing too high leaves the house sitting. Too low leaves money on the table. Read [pricing your Triad home](/guides/pricing-triad-home/).
- **Marketing.** Photos, the listing description, online exposure, signs, and getting in front of buyers' agents.
- **Showings.** Scheduling, screening, and being available.
- **Negotiation.** Offers, counteroffers, the due diligence fee, repair requests after inspection, and appraisal issues.
- **Paperwork.** The state property disclosure, the purchase contract, and coordinating with the buyer's lender and the closing attorney.

## What are the biggest risks?

- **Pricing mistakes.** This is the most expensive one, and it's hard to undo once a listing goes stale.
- **Less exposure.** Without MLS access, fewer buyers and agents may see the house.
- **Disclosure and contract errors.** Mistakes can delay closing or create legal risk. Many FSBO sellers pay a real estate attorney to review documents.
- **Buyer agent pay.** Many buyers have agents, and their offer may ask you to cover that compensation. FSBO doesn't automatically mean zero commission.
- **Time and stress.** Selling is close to a part-time job until closing.

## What do you still pay as an FSBO seller?

You still have seller closing costs, like North Carolina's excise tax, attorney fees, prorated taxes, and your mortgage payoff. See [closing costs in North Carolina](/guides/closing-costs-north-carolina/) and [selling a house with a mortgage](/guides/selling-house-with-mortgage-nc/).

## What are your other options?

- **List with an agent.** You get pricing data, marketing, negotiation, and someone coordinating the deal.
- **Sell as-is.** If the house needs work and you want simplicity, compare listing as-is with a direct offer. Read [selling a house that needs repairs](/guides/selling-house-needs-repairs-nc/).
