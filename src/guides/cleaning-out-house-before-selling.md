Start a cleanout by protecting what matters, then decide what needs to leave. An empty house can be easier to evaluate, but a rushed cleanout can create its own expensive problems.

## What should you protect before removing anything?

Set aside documents, valuables, personal keepsakes, and items whose ownership is uncertain. In an inherited-property situation, confirm authority and family decisions before disposal. Take a record of the rooms and items when useful.

## How do you estimate the work?

Walk the property and separate keeping, donating, selling, and disposal tasks. Get written estimates that describe labor, removal, disposal, and any extra fees. Ask what items the provider won’t handle.

## Should you clean out before choosing a sale path?

Compare the cleanout cost with the requirements of each sale option. A listing and an as-is offer may handle contents differently. Put any contents agreement in writing instead of assuming everything can stay.

## What should happen after the cleanout?

Evaluate condition, access, and preparation after the space is clear. A cleanout can reveal repair issues that weren’t visible. Update your plan before committing to photographs or a listing date.
