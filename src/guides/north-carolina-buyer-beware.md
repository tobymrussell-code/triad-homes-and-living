Use “buyer-beware” as a reminder to investigate the property, not as a complete statement of the law. Buyers, sellers, and brokers can have different duties. Your attorney should explain how those duties apply to your situation.

## What does a careful buyer investigate?

Investigate condition, boundaries, access, services, restrictions, and intended use before relying on a property description. Build a list of unanswered questions from the disclosures and tour. Assign each question to someone qualified to answer it.

## Does a disclosure replace an inspection?

A disclosure and an inspection answer different questions. Read the available disclosure, then plan the checks appropriate to the property. Ask a specialist when the general inspection cannot resolve an important issue.

## How do deadlines affect the plan?

Schedule records review and inspections around the deadlines in your signed agreement. Allow time to understand findings and get estimates. Learning about a problem after a decision deadline can change your options.

## Who should answer a legal or technical question?

Use a North Carolina attorney for legal questions and a qualified specialist for technical ones. Your agent can coordinate the investigation. Don’t ask a quick internet search to do the work of a survey, inspection, or title review.
