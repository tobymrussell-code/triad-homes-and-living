Choose between Thomasville and Lexington by comparing real addresses, not an abstract list of town advantages. A house in the better-sounding town can still be the wrong house for your week.

## How do you make the comparison fair?

Use the same destinations, ownership budget, and property requirements in both towns. Choose comparable candidate homes and keep a written scorecard. Separate a location advantage from a difference in condition or price.

## Which route should decide the search area?

Give the trips you repeat most often the most attention. Test routes toward your actual work location, whether that is in High Point, Winston-Salem, Lexington, or elsewhere. Check both directions at relevant hours.

## How do services and upkeep change the answer?

Compare the utility setup, parcel responsibilities, and likely maintenance at each property. Verify municipal limits and providers separately. Extra land or an older system may change the cost and effort of ownership.

## What if both towns could work?

Keep both towns in the search and let the property-level checks narrow the options. Write down which tradeoffs you’d accept and which you wouldn’t. You don’t need a town loyalty pledge to buy a house.

## Compare actual addresses side by side

Pick one home in Thomasville and one in Lexington with similar budgets and features. Evaluate commuting at your real departure time, lot utility, repair estimates, tax jurisdiction, services, and your routine destinations. A Lexington mailing address may cover a rural property with different systems than one inside town limits. Neither city is automatically better: the individual property determines the tradeoff.

## How do property taxes compare in Thomasville and Lexington?

Property taxes deserve a closer look than a single citywide number. Davidson County's **published 2025 tax-rate schedule** lists the Thomasville municipal rate at **$0.62 per $100 of taxable value** and the Lexington municipal rate at **$0.65 per $100**. For context, the same schedule lists the Davidson County general rate at **$0.54**, the Lexington school rate at **$0.12**, and the Thomasville school rate at **$0.195** per $100. Specific parcels may have additional applicable districts or assessments.

These are **2025 reference rates, not a guarantee of a 2026 tax bill**. Rates, assessed values, and applicable districts must be verified for the specific property and tax year. Two houses with the same mailing city may sit in different jurisdictions. Always review the parcel with the [Davidson County government](https://www.co.davidson.nc.us/) and the applicable city's tax information. Lexington also publishes [property tax explanations](https://www.lexingtonnc.gov/government/taxes-fees/property-tax-questions).

## Which town is easier for commuting?

Thomasville and Lexington sit fairly close together, but the relevant question is not the distance between downtowns. Your commute depends on the actual driveway, your destination, traffic patterns, and time of travel. If you regularly drive to High Point, Winston-Salem, Greensboro, or another Triad destination, compare door-to-door routes from several homes in both towns.

Try the drive on a typical workday. Pay attention to how long it takes to exit the neighborhood, whether the preferred route includes difficult turns, and whether an alternate route is practical. Use the [Triad commute map checklist](/guides/triad-commute-map/) to score different properties consistently.

## What about home prices and the real cost of ownership?

Neither city's name predicts what a particular house is worth. Compare **recently closed comparable sales**, currently competing listings, square footage, condition, lot utility, restrictions, and expected repairs within the same price range. Price per square foot by itself can miss important differences.

Ask for an address-specific estimate of property taxes, insurance, utilities, HOA charges where applicable, and maintenance. A lower asking price can still carry higher near-term repair costs. Older roofs, drainage concerns, private roads, and well or septic systems deserve an independent review.

## City neighborhoods or more space outside town?

Both searches can include properties with differing settings and service arrangements. Decide whether you prioritize a manageable yard, a larger lot, fewer maintenance responsibilities, storage for equipment, or access to everyday destinations. A home advertised with a Thomasville or Lexington address may be outside municipal limits. Verify the tax jurisdiction, utility providers, legal access, and any restrictions **for each parcel**, not from its mailing address.

## A practical way to decide

Make a shortlist of two or three homes in each area and score them against the same criteria:

- Commute to the places you actually visit every week.
- Total monthly ownership costs, not simply the listing price.
- Condition, inspection concerns, and foreseeable repairs.
- Lot use, parking, outdoor storage, and maintenance effort.
- Utility service, restrictions, and any applicable HOA rules.
- How the home itself fits your plans.
