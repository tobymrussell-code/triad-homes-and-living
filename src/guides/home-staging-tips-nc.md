Staging isn't about making your house look like a magazine. It's about making it easy for buyers to picture themselves living there, starting with the listing photos. Staging won't fix a price that's too high, but it helps a well-priced home stand out from the competition.

Here's what I tell sellers around Thomasville, Lexington, and the Triad.

## 1. Declutter first

This is the cheapest, most effective step. Clear countertops, thin out closets and bookshelves, and remove extra furniture so rooms feel bigger. If a house is truly full, start with my [cleanout guide](/guides/cleaning-out-house-before-selling/). Buyers open closets and cabinets, so those count too.

## 2. Depersonalize

Pack away most family photos, collections, and very specific decor. You want buyers looking at the house, not your stuff.

## 3. Maximize light

Open blinds, clean windows, replace dead bulbs, and use consistent warm-white bulbs throughout. Turn on every light before showings and photos. Dark rooms photograph small.

## 4. Go neutral where it counts

You don't have to repaint everything, but bold colors in main rooms can distract buyers. Soft neutrals photograph well and let buyers imagine their own furniture.

## 5. Give every room a clear purpose

If the spare room is a catch-all, stage it as an office, guest room, or whatever best fits the house. Buyers shouldn't have to guess. One note on bedrooms: don't stage or advertise a room as a bedroom if the septic permit doesn't support it. See [selling with well and septic](/guides/selling-well-septic-davidson-county/).

## 6. Focus on the rooms buyers care about most

The living room, kitchen, primary bedroom, and front entry lead the photos and shape first impressions. Put your effort there before secondary rooms.

## 7. Deep clean everything

Baseboards, grout, light fixtures, vents, and windows. A truly clean house tells buyers it's been cared for.

## 8. Fix the smell

Pets, smoke, and mustiness are big turnoffs. Air the house out, clean soft surfaces, and skip heavy air fresheners, which make buyers wonder what you're covering up.

## 9. Boost curb appeal

Mow, edge, trim shrubs, add fresh mulch, clean the front door and porch, and make sure exterior lights work. The front of the house is the first listing photo.

## 10. Walk through like a buyer

Come in through the front door and notice what you'd notice if you'd never been there: sticking doors, burned-out bulbs, clutter on the stairs. Fix the small stuff.

## How should you stage a house in winter?

Winter buyers are often serious ones. Keep walks and the driveway clear and safe, turn on outdoor lights before evening showings, keep the house comfortably warm, and use soft throws and warm lighting to make rooms feel inviting. Photos taken on a gray day can look flat, so schedule photography for the brightest part of the day.
