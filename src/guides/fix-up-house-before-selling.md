Before you spend money preparing a Triad home for sale, compare the likely result in its current condition with targeted preparation and a larger renovation. Start with the costs, the time involved and the amount you could keep at closing.

[Request my CMA and repair review](/seller-improvement-plan/)

## Which improvements have the best resale return?

Research checked November 18, 2026. The latest editions verified for this comparison were the **2025 Zonda Cost vs. Value Report** and the **April 2025 NAR/NARI Remodeling Impact Report**. The regional figures below cover the South Atlantic. A current Winston-Salem-specific table could not be verified.

| Project | Zonda national | Zonda South Atlantic | NAR/NARI national |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Garage-door replacement | 267.7% | 227.7% | — |
| Steel front-door replacement | 216.4% | 219.0% | 100% |
| Manufactured stone veneer | 207.9% | 197.2% | — |
| Fiber-cement siding replacement | 113.7% | 119.2% | — |
| Minor kitchen remodel or upgrade | 112.9% | 109.2% | 60% |
| Vinyl-window replacement | 75.5% | 82.6% | 74% |
| Bathroom remodel or renovation | 80% | 75.3% | 50% |

Figures are estimated **cost recovery**. The studies define projects differently. A dash indicates that the project is absent from NAR's cost-recovery chart. Both reports use professional estimates of resale value. Their percentages provide a starting point for a property-specific discussion.

Sources for the comparison are [Zonda's national table](https://www.jlconline.com/cost-vs-value/2025/national/), the [South Atlantic table](https://www.jlconline.com/cost-vs-value/2025/south-atlantic/), and the [2025 NAR/NARI report](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-04/2025-remodeling-impact-report_04-09-2025.pdf).

## What does cost recovery mean for your money?

Suppose a project costs $10,000 and adds an estimated $8,000 to resale value. That is 80% cost recovery and a $2,000 shortfall before extra holding or selling expenses. At 100%, estimated added value equals project cost. Above 100%, the estimated added value exceeds that cost. Your actual result depends on the house, neighborhood, buyer demand and work completed.

## Which updates would I price first?

My approach for a seller preparing to list is to price work that addresses a visible or practical problem, then compare it against the homes buyers will consider alongside yours.

- **Paint where needed.** Start with worn, damaged or distracting finishes. In NAR's 2025 survey, 50% of Realtors recommended whole-home painting and 41% recommended painting a room before selling. Those are recommendation rates. A paint-return percentage is unavailable in that report.
- **An inviting entrance.** Evaluate a worn door, garage door, exterior lighting and the approach to the house. A door already in good condition may need little work.
- **Landscape cleanup.** Price mowing, pruning, weeding and mulch appropriate to the property before a larger landscaping project.
- **A functional kitchen refresh.** Compare repairs, hardware, lighting and finish updates with the cost of replacing an otherwise usable kitchen.
- **Targeted bathroom work.** Evaluate damaged surfaces, fixtures and lighting before committing to a complete renovation.

NAR's separate **2023** outdoor study estimates 217% recovery for six seasonal lawn treatments and 104% for its defined landscape-maintenance project. These older estimates provide context for curb appeal. A single mowing is a different scope. [Read the outdoor research](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/home-and-design/5-outdoor-projects-that-pay-off-the-most).

## How much work is a minor kitchen remodel?

Zonda's defined project retains cabinet boxes but replaces fronts and hardware, countertops, some appliances, flooring and finishes in a dated kitchen. Its 2025 national estimate is about $28,500. Your cabinet-painting quote needs its own comparison. [Read the project specification](https://www.jlconline.com/cost-vs-value/minor-kitchen-remodel-midrange/).

## How do I compare repairs with selling as is?

Build three estimates for the same house.

| Sale path | Costs to include | Property evidence to compare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current condition | Basic preparation, selling expenses, concessions and holding costs | Similar dated or fixer sales |
| Targeted preparation | Written quotes, contingency, extra time and selling expenses | Sales with comparable targeted updates |
| Larger renovation | Complete scope, permits where needed, contingency, holding and selling costs | Sales with similar quality and extent of renovation |

Use the same mortgage-payoff basis for each scenario. Compare estimated net, cash needed upfront, time and risk. A higher sale price can still leave you with less after the work.
