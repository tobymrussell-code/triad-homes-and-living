Choose repairs by what they solve, not by how satisfying the before-and-after photo might be. A pre-sale project should earn its place in the plan.

## Which work should you evaluate first?

Evaluate safety concerns, unresolved defects, and issues that may affect financing or buyer confidence before cosmetic upgrades. Ask qualified professionals about the scope. Don’t hide a problem behind fresh paint.

## How do you compare the cost with the benefit?

Get written estimates and compare sale scenarios after the cost and time of work. Ask your agent for relevant current property comparisons. Treat an expected benefit as an estimate, not a guaranteed return.

## What can make a repair plan grow unexpectedly?

Hidden conditions, scheduling, materials, and permit questions can change a project. Ask contractors about exclusions and possible change orders. Keep a reserve and a realistic timeline rather than committing every dollar to the first estimate.

## When might selling in current condition make sense?

Compare a current-condition listing or as-is offer when preparation costs and timing conflict with your priorities. Read the written terms and compare likely net proceeds. The right answer depends on your property and situation.
