The hardest part of relocating to the Triad isn't the boxes. It's the order you do things in. People move to Thomasville, Lexington, Winston-Salem, High Point, Greensboro, and the small towns around Davidson County every month, and the ones with a smooth landing all have one thing in common: they planned backward from move-in day.

Here's the checklist I give relocation clients, laid out by when to do each thing.

## What's the smartest first step?

Pick the right part of the map before you pick a house. The Triad isn't one city. It's a cluster of towns that each feel different, and the right one depends on your commute, budget, and whether you want sidewalks or elbow room.

Many people lock onto the town name tied to their new job and never look two exits down the highway. If you're moving for work in Lexington, for example, Thomasville, Welcome, Wallburg, rural Davidson County, Winston-Salem, and High Point are all reasonable commutes, and they aren't priced or paced the same. Start with my [Triad commute map guide](/guides/triad-commute-map/).

## Two to three months out: the map and the money

- Talk to a lender and get pre-approved so you know your real number.
- Learn the towns: drive times to work, the feel of each area, and whether you want in-town convenience or land.
- If you're selling a house where you live now, start that conversation too. Read [selling before you buy](/guides/sell-before-you-buy-nc/).

## One to two months out: narrow it down

- Pick your top two or three areas and tour them in person or virtually.
- Line up movers or a truck, and plan for pets and specialty items.
- If your kids are changing schools, gather records and confirm the school assignment for specific addresses.
- Get a local agent working for you. Good homes don't sit around waiting.

## The final few weeks: lock the logistics

- Confirm your closing or lease dates.
- Set up electric, water, and internet. Schedule internet early, because it's always the holdup.
- File your change of address with the post office.
- Remember that North Carolina gives new residents a limited window to switch their driver's license and vehicle registration, so check the NCDMV deadline now.

## Moving week and after

- Confirm mover time, closing time, and who has the keys the day before.
- After you land, get your NC driver's license and plates, register to vote, and update your address everywhere.
- Find your people: a doctor, a dentist, and the good barbecue spot.

The first two weeks always feel like chaos. Then one morning it just feels like home.

## Which Triad town should you live in?

It depends on what you're optimizing for: price, commute, or lifestyle.

- **Thomasville** is the practical middle, within reach of Lexington, High Point, and Winston-Salem. See the [Thomasville area guide](/areas/thomasville-nc/).
- **Lexington** is a small city with character and real barbecue. Read [moving to Lexington NC](/guides/moving-to-lexington-nc/).
- **Winston-Salem** is the bigger-city option with hospitals, arts, and the most to do.
- **High Point** sits close to Greensboro and the furniture world.
- **Welcome and Wallburg** lean rural and quiet.
- **Rural Davidson County** still has land and space.

For a side-by-side, read [Thomasville vs Winston-Salem vs High Point](/guides/thomasville-winston-salem-high-point/). If you're unsure where you'll land, renting first can help. See [renting vs buying in Davidson County](/guides/renting-vs-buying-davidson-county/).

## What surprises out-of-state buyers about North Carolina?

This isn't legal advice, just how it plays out in real life:

- **Buyer-beware.** Sellers can answer "No Representation" on disclosure items, so do your own homework and get an inspection.
- **Buyer agency agreement.** You'll sign one before your agent shows you homes.
- **Due diligence.** You negotiate a window to inspect, appraise, and finalize financing. You can usually walk away during it, but the due diligence fee generally isn't refundable. Read [due diligence fees in NC](/guides/due-diligence-fees-nc/).
