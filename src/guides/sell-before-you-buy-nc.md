Selling one house and buying the next is one of the most stressful spots in real estate. You can't buy until you free up money from this house, and you don't want to sell and end up with nowhere to go. I get this question all the time from homeowners in Thomasville, Lexington, and across Davidson County.

There are practical ways to make it work. There's just no magic button, so let's talk through the real options.

## Do you actually have to sell before you buy?

For most people, financially, yes. If you need your current home's equity for the next down payment, or you can't qualify to carry two mortgages, selling first or lining the two up tightly is usually the reality.

But having to sell first and having to move out before you find the next place are two different things. The whole game is sequencing: lining up the sale and the purchase so you aren't stuck owning two houses, or none.

## What are your options for selling and buying at the same time?

The right path depends on your equity, your budget, and how much risk you can stomach.

- **Sell first, then buy.** You list, sell, and schedule your next purchase to close right after. It's the cleanest option financially because you know exactly what you have to work with. The catch is timing.
- **Make your offer contingent on your sale.** Your offer says you'll buy once your house sells. It protects you but is weaker in a competitive situation, and some sellers with other buyers won't accept it. It tends to work better in quieter price ranges.
- **Buy first with a bridge loan or a second loan.** If you qualify, you buy the next house and sell yours afterward. The move is easier, but you carry two payments for a while. Talk to a lender before you count on this one.
- **Sell as-is for a predictable close.** If your house needs work or you want certainty on timing, an as-is sale can take out a lot of guesswork. It usually trades some price for speed and certainty. See [selling a house that needs repairs in NC](/guides/selling-house-needs-repairs-nc/).

## How do you avoid owning two houses, or none?

Build flexibility into the contracts instead of hoping the timing works out. In plain English, negotiate the dates.

Two tools do most of the work. A **rent-back**, also called a seller's possession agreement, lets you sell your house and stay in it for a short, agreed period after closing while you finish buying. On the other side, you can negotiate a **later closing date** on your purchase to give your sale time to wrap up. Line those up and you can often go straight from one house to the next without a hotel-and-storage-unit chapter in between.

This isn't legal advice. The exact terms go into the contracts, which is why it helps to have one person coordinating both sides.

## What North Carolina rules matter when you buy the next house?

- **Buyer-beware.** The homework on your next home is on you, so inspections and due diligence matter. Read the [NC buyer-beware guide](/guides/north-carolina-buyer-beware/).
- **Disclosures can say "No Representation."** You can't rely on the disclosure form alone to tell you about a home's condition.
- **Buyer agency agreement.** You'll sign one before an agent shows you homes. See [buyer agency agreements in NC](/guides/buyer-agency-agreements-nc/).

## Should you rent in between?

Sometimes a short rental is the lowest-stress option, especially if you want to sell without pressure and then shop calmly. It means moving twice, but for some people the peace of mind is worth it.
