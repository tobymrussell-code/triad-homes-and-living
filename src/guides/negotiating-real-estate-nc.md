Negotiation isn't about winning an argument. It's about getting to terms that work, without losing the house or your sanity. Here's how it works in North Carolina, where the contract has a few moving parts other states don't.

## What can you negotiate in a North Carolina deal?

More than price:

- **Price**
- **The due diligence fee and earnest money**
- **Closing date and possession,** including a rent-back for the seller
- **Seller-paid closing costs,** called concessions
- **Repairs or credits** after inspection
- **What stays,** like appliances, a shed, or window treatments

Sometimes the winning offer isn't the highest price. It's the one with the cleanest terms and the timing the seller needs.

## Tips for buyers

1. **Do your homework.** Know what similar homes actually sold for, not what they were listed at.
2. **Use the due diligence fee strategically.** In North Carolina, a stronger due diligence fee can make your offer stand out because the seller keeps it if you walk away. Only offer what you could afford to lose. Read [due diligence fees in NC](/guides/due-diligence-fees-nc/).
3. **Don't trash the house.** Sellers are often emotionally attached. Keep repair requests factual and professional.
4. **Negotiate the big items after inspection.** Focus on roof, structure, moisture, systems, and safety, not every loose screw. See [home inspection in North Carolina](/guides/home-inspection-north-carolina/).
5. **Consider credits instead of repairs.** A credit or price reduction lets you control who does the work and how well.
6. **Ask about closing cost help** when the market allows. See [closing costs in North Carolina](/guides/closing-costs-north-carolina/).

## Tips for sellers

1. **Price it right from the start.** A well-priced home gives you more leverage than an overpriced one that sits. See [pricing your Triad home](/guides/pricing-triad-home/).
2. **Look past the top number.** Compare due diligence money, financing strength, closing date, and contingencies.
3. **Know your bottom line before offers arrive.** Figure out your net, not just the sale price.
4. **Use concessions to protect your price.** Sometimes covering some buyer costs keeps the price where you need it.
5. **Respond to repair requests with options.** Fix it, credit it, or decline it, and know which items a lender may require.

## For both sides

- **Control your emotions.** Lean on the numbers and your agent's read of the market.
- **Be clear about priorities.** If timing matters more than price, say so early.
- **Have a Plan B.** Buyers should have other homes in mind. Sellers should know when to wait for a better offer.
- **Know when to walk away.** Overpaying or accepting an offer that doesn't work hurts longer than losing one deal.
