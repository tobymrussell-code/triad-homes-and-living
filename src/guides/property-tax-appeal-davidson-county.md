Davidson and Guilford counties both rolled out new property values effective January 1, 2026, and a lot of homeowners were surprised by the numbers. If you think your value is off, you can appeal. You just need the right evidence and you have to stay inside the county's deadlines.

This isn't legal or tax advice. It's how the process works in real life, from someone who prices homes for a living.

## What is a property tax revaluation?

North Carolina counties must reappraise real property at least every eight years, and many do it more often. The county estimates the market value of every parcel as of a set date, January 1 of the revaluation year, using sales data and the property details it has on file.

The key point is that the county values homes in bulk. Appraisers usually aren't walking through your house. If your property has an aging roof, foundation problems, a dated interior, or wrong details on file, the value can be off.

## How does the appeal process work in North Carolina?

There are generally three levels:

1. **Informal review.** You ask the county tax office to take another look and send your evidence. Many issues get fixed here, especially wrong square footage or room counts.
2. **Board of Equalization and Review.** If the informal review doesn't resolve it, you file a formal appeal with the county Board of Equalization and Review before its deadline. The board hears both sides.
3. **North Carolina Property Tax Commission.** If you disagree with the board's decision, you can appeal to the state commission in Raleigh within the time stated in the board's decision letter.

Deadlines vary by county and year, and they're printed on your notice and the county's tax website. For example, Guilford County's formal appeal deadline for the 2026 reappraisal was May 15, 2026. Always check the current deadline directly with your county.

## What evidence wins an appeal?

"My taxes went up too much" isn't evidence. Value evidence is:

- **Recent comparable sales** of similar homes nearby, as close to the valuation date as possible
- **Wrong property details,** like square footage, bedroom or bath count, or a garage you don't have
- **Photos of condition problems** the county couldn't see
- **Contractor estimates** for needed repairs that affect market value

A realistic market value analysis from a local agent helps here. It's the same work as [pricing a home to sell](/guides/pricing-triad-home/). An appraisal can help too. See [how home appraisals work](/guides/home-appraisals-thomasville-nc/).

## Value vs tax bill: what's the difference?

Your appeal is about **value**, not the bill. Your bill is the value times the county rate, plus a city rate if you're inside city limits. Rates are set separately each year, so a value change and a bill change aren't the same thing. When you're comparing homes to buy, run the real taxes on each address.
