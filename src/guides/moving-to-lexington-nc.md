If you’re new to Lexington, start at **2 North Main Street**. That’s the visitor center in Uptown, which is what people here call downtown. From that corner you can walk to an old courthouse, a neighborhood grocery that sells pimento cheese, a coffee shop, and a candy store. Get back in the car and you can be at a boat ramp on High Rock Lake in about 26 minutes or in downtown Winston-Salem in about 30. Those are three very different ways to spend a day, and the house you pick will change which one feels easy. [Visit Lexington](https://www.visitlexingtonnc.com/) · [City of Lexington](https://www.lexingtonnc.gov/live/about-the-city)

I’m Toby Russell, a local REALTOR® based in neighboring Thomasville. If we had one afternoon to get you oriented, I’d spend part of it driving your normal week. Where will you buy groceries after work? Can you get a second car into the driveway? Would you still like the house after driving home from the lake in the dark? This guide gives you a starting point for those decisions, whether you’re visiting this weekend or planning a move from across the country.

## Get oriented before you tour houses

Lexington is the Davidson County seat in North Carolina’s Piedmont Triad. Here’s how I’d explain the roads to someone who just got here.

- **Uptown** is the Main Street and Center Street area. Start here for the historic courthouse, independent shops, and a short walk between stops.
- **US 52** is the road to pay attention to if you’re going north toward Welcome and Winston-Salem. Check how you get onto it from the driveway. A house can look close to the highway on a map and still take a few turns to reach it.
- **I-85** is the regional route toward Thomasville, High Point, and Greensboro in one direction and Salisbury in the other. Follow the entrance your navigation gives you, since addresses around Lexington can put you near different ramps.
- **NC 8 and the Southmont side** are a good introduction to High Rock Lake. Before comparing lake houses, pick the ramp or marina you’d actually use and drive there from each house.
- **NC 109** is a useful road to recognize when comparing Wallburg with the Lexington, Thomasville, and Winston-Salem sides of the county. A Wallburg property can carry a Winston-Salem mailing address. [NCDOT state transportation map](https://www.ncdot.gov/travel-maps/maps/Pages/state-transportation-map.aspx) · [Town of Wallburg](https://townofwallburg.com/contact-info/)

The first thing I look up is the parcel. A listing can say “Lexington” and be on an Uptown street, in a newer subdivision beyond city limits, or on a county road near the lake. Pull up the property in the county GIS, then verify municipal limits, school assignment, and utility providers separately. You’ll use each answer for a different part of the buying decision. [Davidson County parcel search](https://webgis.co.davidson.nc.us/davidsongis/)

## Compare Lexington and the surrounding areas

### Uptown and established Lexington streets

Start here if you want to try out an in-town routine. Walk the block around the house and then walk to a place you’d use. Check the sidewalks and crossings along the whole route. Look at parking for a second car or guests, whether a delivery truck can reach the house, and how close the home sits to a rail crossing or busy road. At an older house, ask what has been updated, when the work was done, and whether permits were needed. Pay particular attention to crawlspace moisture, drainage, electrical and plumbing updates, and the age of the roof and HVAC.

### Newer subdivisions and the edge of town

Check both the subdivision entrance and the route you’d use for a grocery run. A quiet street inside a development may feed onto a busy road with a tricky left turn. Ask the builder or seller for the lot plan, where future phases may go, whether the street has been accepted for public maintenance, and who maintains stormwater features or common areas. Get the actual HOA dues, restrictions, warranty terms, and final price including lot premiums and upgrades. [New construction near Lexington](/guides/new-construction-near-lexington-nc/) · [Buying with an HOA in NC](/guides/buying-home-with-hoa-nc/)

### Welcome, Midway, and Wallburg

Put these on the list if your week pulls you north as well as into Lexington. Welcome is a straightforward place to test the US 52 side. Midway is another north-of-Lexington starting point. Wallburg gives you a different route via NC 109. To compare any two, run the trips to both workplaces or your other regular stops. Check the return drive too, especially if your shift ends after dark. [Moving to Wallburg and Welcome](/guides/moving-to-wallburg-welcome-nc/)

### Thomasville, Archdale, and the High Point side

I’d include Thomasville if your household is splitting time between Lexington and High Point, or if you want another Davidson County town to compare. Archdale and High Point are worth testing when more of your week takes you northeast. Compare the same kind of house and lot in each area and account for condition, utility setup, and the drive to the highway before drawing conclusions about price. [Thomasville vs. Lexington](/guides/thomasville-vs-lexington-nc/) · [Archdale area guide](/areas/archdale-nc/)

### Southmont, Linwood, Tyro, and rural Davidson County

Look here if your wish list starts with a larger lot, a workshop, or being close to the water. Confirm where the driveway and property boundaries actually run, who maintains the road, whether internet can be installed, and how far your grocery and medical trips would be. Bring a tape measure if you’re moving with a trailer, camper, or boat. Pay attention to driveway grade, gate width, and turnaround space. Treat any plan for a shop, extra bedroom, or second dwelling as a separate question for the permitting authority and your closing attorney. [Davidson County planning and zoning permits](https://www.co.davidson.nc.us/227/Planning-Zoning-Permits) · [Davidson County real estate guide](/areas/davidson-county-nc/)

## Where to eat, from barbecue to a night out

### Start with Lexington-style barbecue

Lexington-style barbecue is pork shoulder, and the local vinegar-based sauce is called **dip**. If the menu offers chopped or sliced, that’s how the meat is served. Try red slaw and hush puppies with it. If you’re comparing barbecue places, order the same basic plate or sandwich at two stops so you can taste the difference. The city’s tourism office keeps a list that includes places outside Uptown too. [Visit Lexington’s barbecue guide](https://www.visitlexingtonnc.com/barbecue)

- **Lexington Barbecue, 100 Smokehouse Lane.** A good first stop for a chopped pork plate and a chance to try the local dip. [Restaurant and menu](https://www.lexbbq.com/)
- **Barbecue Center, 900 N. Main St.** Start with barbecue and split a banana split if you still have room. The restaurant’s roots were in a dairy and ice cream shop. [Barbecue Center](https://barbecuecenter.net/)
- **Speedy’s Barbecue, 408 Piedmont Dr.** Another in-town stop. When searching, use the street address. **Speedy Lohr’s** is a different barbecue restaurant on NC 8. [Visit Lexington’s barbecue listings](https://www.visitlexingtonnc.com/barbecue)

### Coffee, lunch, and dinner around Uptown

- **Perfect Blend Coffee Co., 1 N. Main St.** An easy first stop if you’re starting your walk around the square. [Local dining directory](https://www.visitlexingtonnc.com/local-eats)
- **Conrad & Hinkle, 6 N. Main St.** A local grocery stop for pimento cheese to take home. Walk a little farther up the block to **The Candy Factory, 15 N. Main St.**, for an old-fashioned candy shop. [Conrad & Hinkle](https://conrad-hinkle.com/contact/) · [The Candy Factory](https://lexingtoncandyfactory.com/directions/)

- **Goose and the Monkey Brewhouse, 401 S. Railroad St.** A Depot District stop for local beer, espresso, and food. Check the kitchen’s hours when planning a meal. [Goose and the Monkey](https://gooseandthemonkeybrewhouse.com/)

Check reservations and group rules if people are coming with you. [Childress Vineyards visitor information](https://childressvineyards.com/visiting/)

## What to do in Lexington and within about 45 minutes

### Start close to home

- **An Uptown walk.** Start at the square and visit the Davidson County Historical Museum inside the 1858 courthouse. Browse the shops on Main Street and leave time to get a bite. The museum has its own hours, so check ahead if you’re coming on a Sunday or holiday. [Davidson County Historical Museum](https://www.davidsoncountyhistoricalmuseum.com/) · [Uptown shopping](https://www.visitlexingtonnc.com/historic-uptown)
- **Finch Park, 15 Paul Beck Rd.** About **5 minutes** from Uptown. A handy place to try a short walk, take a picnic, use the playground, or explore the bike trails. Check the city’s park information before visiting after a stretch of rain. [Finch Park](https://www.lexingtonnc.gov/Home/Components/FacilityDirectory/FacilityDirectory/29/727)
- **Washington Park and Lexington Aquatic Park.** The splash pad is at Washington Park, **100 Bookington Dr.** The seasonal pool is at Lexington Aquatic Park, **207 Forest Rose Dr.** They’re two separate stops. [City splash pad and pool information](https://www.lexingtonnc.gov/city-services/parks-and-recreation/splash-pad-and-pool)
- **Lake Thom-A-Lex Park, 700 Yokley Rd.** About **15 minutes** from Uptown. Go for a picnic, playground, fishing, or a paddle. The county posts seasonal hours and separate information for kayak rentals, launching, and fishing. [Lake Thom-A-Lex Park](https://www.co.davidson.nc.us/facilities/facility/details/Lake-ThomALex-Park-11)
- **High Rock Lake, Southmont access.** About **26 minutes** to the wildlife access from Uptown. Use the Southmont access or another confirmed public ramp when planning to launch. If you’re bringing a boat, check water levels and ramp status before towing it out. [NC Wildlife boating access map](https://www.ncwildlife.gov/boating/boating-access-areas) · [High Rock Lake levels](https://cubecarolinas.com/lake-levels/) · [NC Wildlife ramp closures](https://www.ncwildlife.gov/connect/ncwrc-operations-closings-and-alerts/boating-access-area-full-and-partial-closings)

The **Barbecue Festival** brings vendors, music, and barbecue to Uptown in October. Use the event’s date and parking page if you plan to come. ## What changes from one Lexington address to the next

1. **City limits and the tax bill.** Look up the parcel ID and the current bill. Confirm the taxing jurisdictions and whether a city or district charge applies. Ask for an estimate for your situation if the current owner has a special valuation or exemption, the house is newly built, or the tax record appears to cover land only. [County GIS](https://webgis.co.davidson.nc.us/davidsongis/) · [Davidson County tax bill search](https://taxsearch.co.davidson.nc.us/TaxBillSearch)
2. **Utilities and trash.** Confirm electricity, water, sewer or septic, and how the home is heated. Lexington Utilities operates electric, gas, water, and sewer systems, with service availability by address. Davidson Water is another provider in the area. Ask which company or agency serves that lot and whether any tap, hookup, or deposit is due. For city waste service, use the address lookup for collection day and the recycling calendar for your week. Check separately how bulk items and yard debris are handled. [Lexington Utilities](https://www.lexingtonnc.gov/utilities) · [Davidson Water contact listing](https://business.thomasvillechamber.net/list/member/davidson-water-inc-1676) · [City recycling and waste](https://www.lexingtonnc.gov/utilities-services/recycling-waste)
3. **Well and septic.** Ask Davidson County Environmental Health for available permits and records. Compare the permitted bedroom count with the layout you’re buying, and find out where the tank, drain field, and any replacement area sit before planning a pool, garage, or addition. Arrange the appropriate inspections and water testing. [County on-site water protection](https://www.co.davidson.nc.us/206/On-Site-Water-Protection) · [Buying with well and septic in Davidson County](/guides/buying-well-septic-davidson-county/)
4. **School assignment.** Contact the district with the full street address and grade level. Homes with Lexington mailing addresses can fall under Lexington City Schools or Davidson County Schools. Ask the district to confirm assignment, enrollment steps, and any transportation questions that affect your schedule. [Lexington City Schools enrollment](https://www.lexcs.org/page/enrollment-info) · [Davidson County Schools](https://www.davidson.k12.nc.us/)
5. **Internet, mobile service, and access.** Look up the address on the FCC map, then ask the provider for an installation date and any costs to extend service to the house. Test your phone in the house and in the driveway. For a shared or private drive, ask your closing attorney to review the recorded access and maintenance terms. [How to use the FCC broadband map](https://help.bdc.fcc.gov/hc/en-us/articles/10467446103579-How-to-Use-the-FCC-s-National-Broadband-Map)
6. **The monthly cost.** Compare the full payment and your own utility, insurance, HOA, and maintenance estimates. For example, an older in-town house with a pending roof replacement and a rural home with a long private drive can each add costs that won’t appear in the list price. Ask for recent utility bills and get an address-specific insurance quote early. [Davidson County home budget guide](/guides/how-much-house-can-i-afford-davidson-county/)

## High Rock Lake has its own buying checklist

Take a look from the water or a public access if you can. A picture can show a beautiful view and tell you very little about how shallow the cove gets when the water is down. Ask to see the property survey and the pier license, then check with **Cube Yadkin** about the license status, transfer requirements, and what work would need approval. If the listing mentions a shared dock, boat slip, or neighborhood ramp, have your closing attorney verify the recorded rights and who pays to maintain it. [Cube Yadkin shoreline management](https://cubecarolinas.com/shoreline-management/) · [Pier license portal](https://pier.cubecarolinas.com/) · [Lake level information](https://cubecarolinas.com/lake-levels/)

Check flood information at the **house and along the driveway or access road**, then get a property-specific insurance quote. Ask Environmental Health about the septic permit and what records are available. Walk from the parking spot to the dock so you know what it’s like carrying a cooler or gear up that hill. Then drive the exact route from the house to the grocery store and hospital. A lake house that works beautifully for a weekend can ask more of you on a workday. [North Carolina Flood Risk Information System](https://fris.nc.gov/) · [Davidson County Environmental Health](https://www.co.davidson.nc.us/205/Environmental-Health)

## The North Carolina offer process for out-of-state buyers

- **Understand who represents you.** Ask for the agency explanation and read the written agreement, including services, term, and how compensation works. If you’ll be touring with an agent who participates in an MLS subject to the NAR practice changes, expect to sign a written buyer agreement before the tour. [NC Real Estate Commission guidance](https://bulletins.ncrec.gov/has-the-world-exploded-the-nar-settlement-commission-law-and-rules/) · [Buyer agency agreements in NC](/guides/buyer-agency-agreements-nc/)
- **Know what cash is at risk before you sign.** The due diligence fee and earnest money are different. Under the commonly used NC residential form, a negotiated due diligence fee is generally paid to the seller and credited at closing. Refund rights and whether you can recover earnest money after terminating depend on the contract and how and when you terminate. Have your agent and closing attorney explain the specific numbers and deadlines before making an offer. [NC Real Estate Commission on due diligence fees](https://bulletins.ncrec.gov/due-diligence-fees-when-are-they-refunded/) · [NC due diligence guide](/guides/due-diligence-fees-nc/)
- **Line up the checks early.** Talk to your lender about timing, get an insurance quote, and schedule inspections early enough to act on what they find. ## A first scouting trip that tells you something

**Friday afternoon.** If you can, get here in time to drive from a sample home to your job or another regular stop. Drive back at the end of the workday, swing into the grocery store you’d actually use, and look at the home’s parking and street lighting after dark.

**Saturday morning.** Start in Uptown for coffee and a walk around the square. See an in-town property and one farther out using the same list of must-haves. For each, record driveway-to-work time, ownership costs, condition, and one thing you’d give up by choosing it. Then get barbecue and spend the afternoon at Finch Park, Lake Thom-A-Lex, or Southmont to see what an ordinary weekend might look like.

**Before you head home.** Choose the side of Lexington that made your regular trips easier, and write down what you still need to verify by address. If your work location or preferred side of town is still up in the air, a short-term rental can give you time to learn the area before committing to a property.
