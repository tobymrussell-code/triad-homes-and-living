Buying your first home is exciting, and it's also a lot of new vocabulary at once. Here's the plain-English version for first-time buyers in Thomasville, Lexington, and the Triad, in the order you'll actually need it.

## Step 1: Get your money ready

- **Know your credit.** Pull your reports and fix errors early.
- **Pay down high-interest debt.** Every monthly payment you eliminate gives you more room for a mortgage.
- **Save for more than the down payment.** You'll also need due diligence money, earnest money, the inspection, closing costs, and a cushion for the first repair.
- **Don't open new credit or switch jobs mid-process** without talking to your lender.

## Step 2: Get pre-approved, not just pre-qualified

A pre-qualification is a quick estimate. A pre-approval means a lender has looked at your income, assets, and credit. It tells you your real number and makes your offer stronger. Then compare that number with what's comfortable. See [how much house can I afford in Davidson County](/guides/how-much-house-can-i-afford-davidson-county/).

## Step 3: Understand your loan options

- **Conventional loans.** Common for buyers with solid credit. With less than 20% down, you'll usually pay private mortgage insurance.
- **FHA loans.** Government-backed, with more flexible credit and down payment requirements.
- **VA loans.** For eligible service members, veterans, and some surviving spouses, often with no down payment.
- **USDA loans.** For eligible properties in rural areas, often with no down payment. Parts of Davidson County may qualify. Read [USDA loans in Davidson County](/guides/usda-loans-davidson-county-nc/).
- **Fixed vs adjustable.** A fixed rate keeps principal and interest the same for the life of the loan. An adjustable rate can change after an initial period.

**Down payment help:** North Carolina has state and local assistance programs, but the rules, income limits, and funding change. Ask your lender which ones you qualify for right now.

## Step 4: Shop the map before the house

Pick your top two or three areas based on your commute, budget, and lifestyle. Your money may go further in Thomasville, Welcome, or rural Davidson County than in parts of Winston-Salem or High Point. Read the [Triad commute map guide](/guides/triad-commute-map/).

## Step 5: Learn the North Carolina buying process

- **Buyer agreement.** You'll sign one before touring homes with an agent. See [buyer agency agreements in NC](/guides/buyer-agency-agreements-nc/).
- **Buyer-beware.** Sellers can answer "No Representation" on disclosures, so inspections matter. Read the [NC buyer-beware guide](/guides/north-carolina-buyer-beware/).
- **Due diligence.** You pay a due diligence fee for a window to inspect and back out. Read [due diligence fees in NC](/guides/due-diligence-fees-nc/).
- **Inspection and appraisal.** Read [home inspection in North Carolina](/guides/home-inspection-north-carolina/).
- **Closing.** An attorney closes the deal. Read [closing costs in North Carolina](/guides/closing-costs-north-carolina/).

## Step 6: Plan for life after closing

Budget for property taxes and insurance changes, set up an emergency fund for repairs, and keep up with routine maintenance like HVAC filters, gutters, and, on rural homes, septic pumping.
