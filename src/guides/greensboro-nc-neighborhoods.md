Greensboro is the largest city in the Triad, and its neighborhoods range from historic districts near downtown to wooded lots up north. Here's a quick buyer's tour of a few well-known areas, plus what to check before you fall for one.

One note before we start: the "best" neighborhood is the one that fits your commute, budget, and lifestyle. I describe places by their housing and setting, not by who lives there.

## Fisher Park

Just north of downtown, built around the park that shares its name. Fisher Park is a National Register historic district with many homes from the late 1800s and early 1900s. You're close to downtown restaurants and events. Historic homes are beautiful, and they come with older systems and, in a historic district, rules for exterior changes. Ask about both before you buy.

## Dunleath

Next to Fisher Park, centered on Sternberger Park. Dunleath has many late-19th and early-20th-century homes and a strong neighborhood-events culture.

## Westerwood

West of downtown, near UNC Greensboro and Greensboro College, and next to Lake Daniel Park and its greenway. Expect Craftsman bungalows, Colonial two-stories, and Tudor-style cottages, many with original details.

## College Hill

Between downtown and UNCG, one of Greensboro's older neighborhoods, with a mix of historic houses and a lively, walkable feel near campus.

## Lindley Park

West of downtown, near the Greensboro Arboretum. Tree-lined streets, single-family homes, and quick access to parks and greenways.

## Old Irving Park

North of Fisher Park, laid out around the Greensboro Country Club. Larger lots and a mix of Colonial, Classical Revival, Tudor, and brick ranch homes.

## Summerfield

A separate town north of Greensboro with rolling hills, woods, and larger lots. It feels rural, but you can drive into the city. If you want land, compare it with rural Davidson County too.

## What should you check before buying in Greensboro?

- **Your commute.** Drive it at your real travel time. See the [Triad commute map guide](/guides/triad-commute-map/).
- **Historic district rules,** if the home is in one.
- **Older-home systems:** roof, electrical, plumbing, crawlspace, and foundation. See [home inspection in North Carolina](/guides/home-inspection-north-carolina/).
- **HOA documents** in newer subdivisions. See [buying a home with an HOA](/guides/buying-home-with-hoa-nc/).
- **Guilford County taxes,** plus city taxes inside city limits. Run the actual address.
