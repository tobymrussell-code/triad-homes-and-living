Closing costs are the part of a real estate deal nobody puts on a yard sign. In North Carolina, closing costs are the fees both buyers and sellers pay, on top of the price of the house, to actually get the deal done. People get caught off guard all the time because they budgeted for the down payment and forgot everything else.

Here's the honest rundown of who pays what, plus the North Carolina rules that trip up people moving here, whether you're buying in Thomasville, selling in Lexington, or landing anywhere in Davidson County and the Triad.

## What are closing costs in North Carolina?

Closing costs are the fees and charges, separate from the sale price, that buyers and sellers pay to finish a home sale. They cover things like loan fees, attorney and title work, taxes, insurance, and prepaid items. The buyer and the seller each have their own set.

Think of the purchase price as the cost of the house and closing costs as the cost of doing the deal. Every line is itemized on a settlement statement before closing, so nobody should be guessing at the table.

## Who pays closing costs, the buyer or the seller?

Both do. Each side covers its own typical set of costs, and who pays what is also negotiable.

**Buyers usually pay:**

- Lender fees and the appraisal
- Title search and title insurance
- The closing attorney
- Recording fees
- Prepaid items like homeowners insurance and property taxes that go into an escrow account

If you're paying cash, you skip the loan-related items but still have the title, attorney, and recording costs.

**Sellers usually pay:**

- The real estate commission they agreed to
- North Carolina's excise tax on the sale
- Their share of prorated property taxes
- The payoff on their existing mortgage

The key word is negotiable. In a slower market, it's common for a buyer to ask the seller to put money toward the buyer's costs. That's called a seller concession. When sellers have multiple offers, you have less room to ask.

## What closing costs are specific to North Carolina?

A few things about closing here surprise out-of-state buyers:

- **Attorneys close the deal.** In many states, a title or escrow company runs the closing. Here, a real estate attorney does the title search, prepares the documents, and conducts the closing. Attorney fees on your statement are normal.
- **The seller pays an excise tax.** North Carolina charges an excise tax, often called revenue stamps, when property changes hands. It's based on the sale price, and the seller typically pays it.
- **The due diligence fee comes early.** Technically it isn't a closing cost. It's money the buyer pays the seller right after going under contract for the right to inspect and investigate. It credits toward your purchase if you close, and you generally don't get it back if you walk away. Read [how due diligence fees work in NC](/guides/due-diligence-fees-nc/).
- **Earnest money is separate.** It's a good-faith deposit that's held and applied at closing. Newcomers mix it up with the due diligence fee all the time.

None of this should scare you off. It's just the North Carolina rulebook, and it works fine once someone walks you through it before you're sitting at the table.

## How much are closing costs in North Carolina?

It depends on the price of the home, your loan, and what the two sides negotiate, so there's no single number that fits every deal. I'm not going to throw a percentage at you. A number that's "close enough" can be off by a lot on your actual deal.

The right move is to get a written estimate before you're deep into a contract. Your lender gives you a Loan Estimate that lays out projected closing costs, and your agent and closing attorney walk you through the rest line by line.

## How do you avoid closing cost surprises?

Get your estimate early, read it line by line, and ask what's negotiable before you sign, not after.

- Ask your lender for a Loan Estimate as soon as you're serious, not the week of closing.
- Budget the due diligence fee and earnest money as separate cash you need early.
- If you're buying in a softer market, ask your agent about a seller concession.
- If you're selling, put the commission and excise tax into your net proceeds math before you fall in love with a list price.
- Pick a closing attorney and agent who explain things instead of just handing you paper to sign.
